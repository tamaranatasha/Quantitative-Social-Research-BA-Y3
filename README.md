# Quantitative Social Research: Was the Brexit vote rooted in English nationalism?

## Overview
A socio-technical research project, developed while undertaking BA Politics & Sociology at the University of Leeds. The project investigates English nationalism within the United Kingdom, regarding the 2016 European Union referendum vote.      

## Objectives
- To what extent does a link exist between English identification and views towards Britain’s policy regarding the EU referendum?   
- Is there a statistically significant link between the English nationalism and the Brexit vote?   

## Data
- British Social Attitudes Survey 2016

## Methodology
- Data cleaning and preprocessing in R
- Exploratory data analysis for categorical data - counts/percentages and bar charts.
- Bivariate analysis methods - crosstabulation tables to analyse the distribution, chi-squared tests to test if there is significant evidence to reject the null hypothesis, clustered and stacked bar charts to represent the data.
- Logistic regression models, dependant variable is binary with two outcomes. 

## Tools & Libraries
- R
- tidyverse
- ggplot2

## Key Findings
- When looking at identification as British or English, 66.2% of people who identified as English voted to leave the EU, whilst 64.4% of people who identified as British voted to remain in the EU. 

## Impact
- Highlighted differences across demographic groups for identification as British or English.
- Identified core themes related to demographics and EU Referendum vote such as; age, education, party identification and immigration concern.

## Reproducibility
- All analysis scripts are provided in this repository. The workflow is fully reproducible using the listed R packages.
